03 · The engagement loop

The Hook, applied to campus events

Each pass through the loop leaves something behind (a profile, a team, a pass) that loads the next trigger.

Goal: move from external triggers (a friend’s WhatsApp link) to an internal one: “what’s on tonight?” at 7 pm.

1 · Trigger

WhatsApp card from a friend · reminder 3 h before · poster QR at SAC · boredom at 7 pm

2 · Action

Returning user: link → Register → pass. 2 taps, under 30 s. Login deferred until it’s needed.

3 · Variable reward

Tribe: “38 from Nilgiri going” · Hunt: tonight’s surprise pick · Self: the pass gets “stamped”

4 · Investment

Profile saved once · teams formed · passes in the wallet · clubs followed

hooked
(Eyal, 2014)

Four loops keep people coming back between fests

Wallet loop

  2. Invites 2–5 teammates
  3. Teammates sign up to join
  4. They browse other events

invite join rate ≥ 50%

Share loop

  1. Rich WhatsApp card: what, when, where, ₹
  2. Friend taps, no login needed
  3. Registers in 2 taps
  4. Shares it onward

K = invites × conversion

Organiser loop

  1. Club publishes on IITD Live
  2. Posts our link on Insta
  3. Students arrive for it
  4. …and find other events

repeat organisers ≥ 60%

Fests bring the traffic. Weekly club events keep it.

03 · Network effects & cold start

A two-sided flywheel,
started by hand

Anchor tenant

Rendezvous is our anchor store

Malls give anchors a ≥ 72% rent discount because they bring everyone else’s traffic. So RDV pays nothing: free tools, an IITD-first pronite window, featured placement.

Atomic network

Dense before wide

Start with DMS + 2–3 hostels and make IITD Live the only place their events are listed. Campus is the perfect size: about 12k students, 2 km, one email domain.

Value with one side empty

Organisers come for the tool: a better Google Form + QR check-in. Students come for a complete calendar: we list every event by hand for the first 6–8 weeks.

04 · At the gate

Scan the QR, check the person, admit. Under 10 seconds.

1

Student opens the pass

Wallet → QR code + 8-character pass code. Works offline at the gate.

2

Checker scans it

Gate screen on a volunteer’s phone: camera, or type the code.

3

System checks the pass

Real pass? This event? Not used yet? Paid?

4

Checker verifies the person

Name, entry no., hostel and IITD badge on screen, matched to their college ID card.

5

Admit

“Name matches, admit”, or collect ₹ first. The pass is marked used and the scan is logged.

If a check fails

  • ✗ Already used: shows when, and by which checker
  • ✗ Different event
  • ✗ Not a valid pass: fake or mistyped
  • ✗ Cancelled or waitlisted
  • ₹ Payment due: collect cash, then admit

name, entry no. and hostel to match against the ID card

every scan, from every checker, lands in one shared log

04 · Identity checks · next step

Is it really them? College ID + Aadhaar

Three levels. Each event uses the lightest one that fits; big fests with outside crowds use the strongest.

live today

1 · IITD email + entry no.

  • Sign in with @iitd.ac.in to earn the IITD seal
  • Checker matches name and entry no. to the IITD ID card

for club events and IITD-only workshops

next

2 · College ID card

  • Outsiders upload a photo of their college ID at sign-up
  • Organiser or admin approves before the pass is issued
  • ID photo shows on the gate screen for a face match

for inter-college competitions and workshops

next

3 · Aadhaar via DigiLocker

  • One-time, consent-based e-KYC (DigiLocker or UIDAI offline e-KYC)
  • Confirms legal name and photo
  • We keep only “verified” and the last 4 digits, never the Aadhaar number or a copy of the card

for RDV pronites and fests that already ask for a govt ID

RDV already asks for a government ID at entry (research/events.csv). Aadhaar e-KYC via DigiLocker needs us to register as a DigiLocker requester. DPDP Act 2023: consent, purpose limit, delete on request.

06 · Business model & go-to-market

IITD students are the audience. Outsiders are the revenue.

What we charge, and to whom

  1. 1Outsider passes: a small convenience fee on fest entry and pronite passes bought by students from DU and other colleges. main source
  2. 2Paid add-ons: a few % on workshops and competition fees, paid by the buyer.
  3. 3Fest tools, later: promoted listings and audience insights.
Why IITD students pay ₹0Most events are free for them, and they spend about ₹148 a semester. Even 5% of all of it is ~₹1 lakh a year. They’re the crowd that makes fests and outsiders come: the subsidised side.
StallsStall fees belong to the fest. We don’t take a cut: we sell their outside tickets and run their gate.

Go-to-market ladder

  1. 1List every event by hand for 6–8 weeks
  2. 2DMS + 2–3 hostels first; hostel ambassadors
  3. 3RDV as anchor: free tools, outsider ticketing
