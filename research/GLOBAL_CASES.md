# IITD Live — What Campus Marketplaces Worldwide Teach Us

Desk research only (no interviews). This replaces primary research with evidence from comparable platforms and from platform-strategy research. It builds on [`FEATURES_GROWTH.md`](FEATURES_GROWTH.md).

Many figures below are company-reported or come from secondary write-ups. Treat them as directional, and say so on the slides.

## 1. Theory: why the professor is right about Rendezvous, and what it implies

| Framework | Finding | Implication for IITD Live |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor tenants**: Pashigian & Gould (1998), *Journal of Law & Economics* | Mall developers give anchor stores a rent discount of **≥72%** vs other tenants. Anchors bring traffic that benefits everyone, so smaller tenants pay a premium. [1] | **RDV is our anchor store.** Don't charge it; subsidise it. Make the money from the "smaller tenants" (club events, workshops, brands) who benefit from RDV's traffic. |
| **Two-sided pricing**: Eisenmann, Parker & Van Alstyne (2006), *HBR* "Strategies for Two-Sided Markets" | Platforms usually subsidise one side. The key question is which side, and for how long. **Marquee users** are often won with exclusivity or incentives that only have value when used on the platform. [2][3] | Subsidy side = **students** (13/24 spend ₹0). Money side = organisers' paid add-ons, outsiders, brands. For RDV, offer in-platform incentives (free tools, featured placement, sponsor intros), not cash. |
| **Cold start / atomic networks**: Andrew Chen (2021), *The Cold Start Problem* | Networks start in small, dense "atomic networks"; Tinder reportedly needed about 500 people on one campus. Big-bang launches (Google+) fail. [4] | Launch dense, not wide: one community at a time. |

## 2. Case studies: who solved this before

### Successes

| Platform (where) | What they did | Lesson → IITD Live action |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook (Harvard, 2004)** | Required a college email. Opened campus by campus: Harvard → Stanford, Columbia, Yale → ~30 by end-2004. Grew by word of mouth, not ads. [5] | IITD email verification is the moat. Win one campus completely before expanding. |
| **Tinder (US campuses, 2012)** | Co-founder pitched sorority chapters, then the matching fraternities, so people saw others they knew on the app. ~5k → 15k users on that tour. Campus parties required **downloading the app to enter**. [6] | **"Entry through the app" events.** An IITD-first pronite window that works only through IITD Live is our version of the Tinder party. |
| **Snackpass (Yale, 2017–19)** | .edu login + **gifting** (send food/points to friends). Reportedly reached 80% of Yale within a semester; ~75% penetration within 6 months on later campuses; ambassadors to expand. [7][8] | Build a social action into the transaction: **"Get a pass for a friend"** and team invites, so each user has a reason to bring others. |
| **Fizz (Stanford → 200+ campuses, 2022–24)** | Paid student ambassadors per shift (~$50–60). Paper slips under dorm doors, sign-up tables, free donuts. **Paid clubs per member who signed up.** Launched at the start of semester, when new students are lost. [9] | Hostel ambassadors. **Pay clubs per verified member sign-up** (small ₹ or free promotion). Launch at freshers' orientation / Speranza. |
| **Rubric (Australia/UK)** | Societies, tickets, memberships, merch and **verified resale** in one app; club execs nominate ticket scanners. Claims >1 in 4 students use it. A student union (UTAS) **requires all clubs to use it**. [10] | **An institutional mandate is the strongest adoption lever.** Ask BRCA / Student Affairs to make IITD Live the default place clubs list events. |
| **FIXR / Fatsoma (UK student nights)** | Student verification + **rep networks**. Fatsoma reps sell via personal links for an organiser-set commission; the platform charges nothing for the rep system. [11][12] | **Referral links with commission for outsider passes.** RDV/Tryst get a free sales force; we get distribution in other colleges. |
| **Partiful (US, social events)** | Every invite is a **link that works without installing an app**. RSVP spreads through iMessage / WhatsApp / Instagram. Core product free; monetise add-ons later. 500K+ MAU, a16z-backed. [13] | The event page must work as a no-install link inside WhatsApp, since 17/24 discover through WhatsApp. Free core; charge only for add-ons. |
| **Eventbrite** | Free events cost nothing. It **added a fee on free events in 2023 and reversed it in 2024**. Fees on paid tickets are passed to buyers by default. [14] | **Never charge for free events.** Put a % fee on paid tickets, paid by the buyer. |
| **Student Seats (US)** | Student-only resale. Verified university email. Escrow; buyer fee ~7%; 4.6★ (383 ratings). [15] | Template for P2 pass transfer: verified IITD users only, escrow, small buyer fee, and only when the organiser allows it. |
| **Unstop (India)** | Students use it free. Revenue from recruiters (reported ₹30 cr+ in FY25). Colleges buy promotion: IIM Kashipur manages **₹3 lakh** of paid Unstop ad inventory. [16] | Indian colleges *do* pay for event promotion, which validates promoted listings. Unstop already hosts fest competitions: **link to it, don't fight it.** |
| **InstiApp (IIT Bombay)** | Built by students (DevCom): event feed, institute calendar, mess menu, QR campus access. ~34k Android downloads. [17] | Proof that a student-built app can become semi-official infrastructure at an IIT. Partner with IITD's student tech body (DevClub) for credibility. |
| **Flipkart Lite (India)** | Went back from app-only to a PWA: 3× time on site, 40% higher re-engagement, **70% higher conversion** from home-screen users; 63% of users on 2G. [18] | **Web-first PWA is the right call** for Indian students. No app-store friction, works on weak hostel Wi-Fi. |

### Failures

| Platform | Why it died | How IITD Live avoids it |
|---|---|---|
| **Ventfull (Harvard)** | Calendar-only aggregator. Upkeep and customer acquisition cost more than it earned. [19] | Don't be *only* a calendar. Organiser tools + paid add-ons from day one; poster auto-extraction keeps upkeep cheap. |
| **Toga (USC)** | Shut down suddenly when a planned acquisition fell through; students lost their shared calendar. [20] | Keep running costs near zero (static hosting, free tiers) so the platform survives the team graduating. Plan a handover to a student body. |
| **Uni-Life (VU Amsterdam)** | The university ended the partnership because few students actively used it. [21] | Don't ask for "yet another app" install. Live inside WhatsApp links and email. |
| **UFV student union app (Canada)** | 800 downloads in year 1; couldn't deliver what was planned; moved to an existing platform. [21] | Ship a small P0. Scope creep kills student-built apps. |
| **Facebook Campus (2020–22)** | College-only network shut in <2 years; Facebook said Groups already served students. [22] | **Don't compete with WhatsApp groups. Plug into them.** |

## 3. Where evidence overrides our survey

| Topic | Our survey said | Wider evidence says | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Friends attending** | Low demand: 3/24 | Foursquare event study (ICWSM 2014): *whether friends attend* is the **dominant** factor in event attendance; popularity is a strong second. [23] People under-report social influence when asked directly. | Upgrade to **P1, privacy-safe**: show aggregates ("38 from Aravali hostel going", "120 IITD students going"), not named friends by default. |
| **No-shows** | 13/24 skipped events after registering | Event check-in data from 1,000+ events: median no-show **~28% for free events vs ~17% for paid**. [24] | Free events: **overbook ~25%** + waitlist auto-release. Optional ₹10 refundable hold for capacity-limited events. |
| **App vs web** | (Not asked) | Flipkart Lite PWA results [18]; Uni-Life and UFV app failures [21] | Web app installable from the browser (PWA); no native app. |
| **Charging fees** | ₹0 to ₹20, split evenly | Eventbrite's free-event fee was reversed after a year [14]; Fatsoma/Eventbrite pass fees to buyers [12][14] | Free events always free. A small % on paid tickets, buyer pays, shown upfront. |

## 4. Answers to our open questions, with no interviews needed

These questions were listed in [`FEATURES_GROWTH.md`](FEATURES_GROWTH.md) §8. These defaults are evidence-backed and low-risk.

1. **How do we get RDV without knowing who decides?**
   Use rung 0 (unofficial guide, public info) now. It needs no permission. The pitch deck is built on the anchor-tenant logic + the RDV 2023 incident: *students holding tickets were stopped at the gates and the concerts cancelled* [25]. That is exactly what IITD-verified QR passes and capacity control prevent.
2. **Can we email all students?**
   Don't depend on it. Distribution = WhatsApp links (Partiful) + hostel ambassadors (Fizz) + paying clubs per sign-up (Fizz).
3. **Can we collect payments?**
   For the MVP, **don't touch money**. Link to the organiser's existing payment page or Unstop. We earn from promoted listings and sponsors first. Start ticketing once one organiser asks for it. This removes the legal and refund risk for a class project.
4. **Class scope?**
   Working web prototype + pitch deck. Use this file as the "secondary research" section, in place of interviews.

## 5. Final recommended playbook

**Product (P0):**
- No-install event links (Partiful)
- IITD-email login (Facebook)
- Feed with eligibility labels
- One-tap + **team** registration
- QR wallet
- WhatsApp/email reminders
- Organiser poster-upload + QR scanner (Rubric's nominated scanners)

**Fest mode (P1):**
- Schedule grid
- Live updates
- **IITD-first pronite window** (Tinder's "app to enter")
- Overbooking + waitlist (no-show data)
- Hostel-level "going" counts (Foursquare study)
- Referral links for outsider passes (Fatsoma reps)

**Growth:**
- Launch at freshers / Speranza (Fizz timing)
- One hostel cluster first (atomic network)
- Hostel ambassadors
- Clubs rewarded per verified sign-up
- Ask BRCA to make listing the default (Rubric/UTAS mandate)

**Money:**
- Free for students and free events (Eventbrite)
- Promoted listings (Unstop/IIM Kashipur)
- Brand sponsorship (Pokémon GO × RDV 2026)
- Later: % on paid tickets, verified resale fee (Student Seats)

**RDV:**
- Treat as anchor tenant: free everything, featured placement, sponsor introductions (Pashigian & Gould)
- Monetise the long tail of events around it
